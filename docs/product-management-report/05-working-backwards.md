# Amazon's Working Backwards PR/FAQ: DramaSnap

## Overview

Amazon's Working Backwards methodology, developed internally and popularized by executives like Ian McAllister and Colin Bryar, requires product teams to write a customer press release and FAQ *before* any development begins. The discipline forces clarity on what you're actually building, who it's for, and why it matters — from the customer's perspective, not the engineer's or PM's.

The process:
1. Write a press release that announces the finished product as if it's already launched successfully
2. Write an external FAQ answering questions customers will ask
3. Write an internal FAQ answering questions stakeholders and skeptics will raise

If you can't write a compelling press release for your product, you don't have a clear enough product vision to begin building.

## External FAQ: Customer Questions

### Q1: What exactly is DramaSnap, and how is it different from Netflix or TikTok?

DramaSnap is a short drama streaming platform where every series consists of 100 episodes, each exactly 60 seconds long. It's different from Netflix because Netflix episodes are 30–60 minutes — designed for TV time, not mobile micro-sessions. It's different from TikTok because TikTok is random discovery content, not serialized storytelling with character arcs and cliffhangers. DramaSnap is specifically designed for the 10–20 minutes of mobile time you have multiple times per day. You can watch 5–8 episodes on your commute and feel like you've genuinely made progress in a story you care about.

### Q2: Is the content any good if it's made by AI?

We understand the skepticism, and we take it seriously. Our content goes through quality review before publishing — we're not just dumping unreviewed AI output into the app. Our writers (human and AI) follow story structure principles that have proven to engage audiences. Our early beta users consistently rate content 4+ stars and report high completion rates. The answer is: yes, the content is good, and we're committed to improving it every month. We'd rather you try it and judge for yourself than take our word for it.

### Q3: Why is the subscription only $6.99/month? Isn't that really cheap?

Our AI production model allows us to produce content at dramatically lower cost than human-produced drama platforms. That cost advantage is something we want to pass on to subscribers. We think drama fans deserve a fair, predictable subscription model — not the coin-per-episode model where a single series can cost $20–$30 to watch in full. Our goal is to be the most affordable premium drama subscription you have.

### Q4: How does personalization work? Is DramaSnap tracking everything I watch?

DramaSnap uses your viewing behavior — what you watch, what you skip, what you rewatch, what you share — to improve recommendations. This is standard practice for all streaming platforms. We do not sell your personal data to third parties. Our personalization is used exclusively to improve your experience on DramaSnap. Over time, you'll notice that the content recommended to you feels increasingly tailored to what you actually enjoy. You can also explicitly set genre and theme preferences in your profile settings.

Yes. DramaSnap Premium subscribers can download series for offline viewing. Up to [X] series can be downloaded at a time. Downloaded episodes expire after 30 days or when your subscription lapses.

### Q7: What genres are available?

At launch, DramaSnap offers content in romance, thriller, supernatural drama, workplace drama, and historical romance. We're expanding into LGBTQ+ romance, horror, comedy drama, and sports drama in Q2–Q3 2026. If there's a genre you'd like to see, you can request it through the app — our AI engine can produce in virtually any genre.

## Internal FAQ: Stakeholder Questions

### Q1: Can AI really produce drama content that's good enough for consumers to pay for?

**Short answer:** Yes, based on beta testing, but with caveats.

**Longer answer:** The quality bar for short drama is not the same as for prestige TV. The format is melodrama — heightened emotions, clear archetypes, fast plot progression. This is a format where AI LLMs actually perform well because the narrative patterns are well-established and the dialogue is stylized rather than naturalistic. Our beta testing shows that users rate AI-produced episodes 4+ stars when the story structure is sound and the visual quality meets a reasonable threshold.

The key risks are:
- Continuity errors across 100 episodes (AI loses context over long arcs) — mitigated by our story bible and continuity-checking pipeline
- Visual quality inconsistency (AI video generation has artifact issues) — mitigated by QA filtering and improving rapidly with model updates
- "Uncanny valley" dialogue — mitigated by human editorial review of scripts before production

This is a solvable engineering and editorial challenge, not a fundamental impossibility.

### Q2: What will this cost to build and operate? What's the unit economics model?

**Production costs (per episode):**
- Current: $500–$800 (compute, API costs, human QA)
- At 10K episodes: $250–$350
- At 100K episodes: $150–$200

**Revenue per subscriber:**
- Premium subscription: $6.99/month
- Ad-supported tier: estimated $2–3 ARPU/month

**Target CAC:** $4–8 (performance marketing to drama enthusiasts)
- Payback period: 1–2 months at premium subscription
- LTV at 12-month retention: $75–84 (premium)

**Break-even analysis:** At 50K paying subscribers, monthly subscription revenue ($350K) covers production costs for the catalog maintenance + base operations. Growth capex is funded separately.

### Q3: What is the legal risk around AI-generated content, copyright, and the writers' unions?

**Copyright risk:** AI-generated content that does not reproduce verbatim copyrighted text is generally not a copyright infringement under current US law (as of early 2026). We use AI to generate original scripts, not to reproduce existing works. Our legal counsel has reviewed this and we maintain standard indemnification policies.

**Writers' Guild / Union risk:** DramaSnap is not a signatory to WGA agreements (we are a startup not a traditional studio). AI-generated content on non-signatory platforms is not currently regulated by guild agreements. We monitor this space carefully and our legal team engages proactively with policy developments.

**Defamation / Content moderation risk:** AI can generate content that is defamatory, offensive, or harmful. We mitigate this through:
- Content policy guidelines embedded in all AI generation prompts
- Automated moderation of generated content before publishing
- Human spot-check review process
- Clear content takedown policy

**Disclosure risk:** There is emerging regulatory interest in requiring disclosure of AI-generated content. We proactively disclose that our content is AI-produced and will comply with any disclosure requirements as they emerge.

### Q4: What is DramaSnap's sustainable competitive moat? Why can't ReelShort just copy this?

Our competitive moat is built on four compounding advantages:

1. **Process Power:** Our AI production pipeline is not a single tool — it is an integrated workflow of model selection, prompt engineering, quality calibration, and distribution automation. Building this pipeline requires 12–18 months of investment that competitors cannot shortcut.

2. **Cornered Resource:** Every episode we produce generates behavioral data (watch time, skip rates, completion, sharing) that trains our personalization and quality models. After 12 months of operation with 500K+ active users, our behavioral dataset is a proprietary asset that no new entrant can replicate without building the user base first.

3. **Counter-Positioning vs. ReelShort:** ReelShort's brand is built on human-produced content with human actors. Switching to AI would require them to acknowledge that their $50,000-per-series production was unnecessary, undermine their audience's sense of what they paid for, and potentially alienate their actor and production relationships.

4. **Scale Economies:** As our production volume increases, per-episode cost decreases. At 100,000 episodes produced, our cost is $150–$200 per episode. A new entrant starting from scratch faces $500–$800/episode. This cost gap compounds as we scale.

### Q5: How do we handle the "AI-generated content quality stigma" risk?

This is a real risk. A meaningful percentage of consumers have a negative prior association with "AI-generated" content. Our strategy:

1. **Don't lead with "AI-generated" in consumer marketing.** Our consumer positioning is "personalized drama designed for mobile," not "AI drama." AI is a backend capability, not the lead brand message.

2. **Let quality speak.** Our best content is the best argument against quality stigma. We identify "signature series" that we invest extra QA in and use as the face of the platform.

3. **Reframe AI as personalization.** "AI-powered" in our messaging refers primarily to personalization (the feed learns your taste) rather than production. Consumers respond positively to personalization framing.

4. **Monitor NPS and sentiment closely.** If AI quality is a driver of negative NPS, we escalate production quality investments immediately.

### Q6: What happens if a major AI model provider (OpenAI, Runway) changes pricing, availability, or terms?

This is a vendor dependency risk. Mitigations:

- **Multi-vendor strategy:** We use multiple AI providers at each stage of the pipeline and maintain the ability to swap providers within 2–4 weeks
- **Own what we can:** For video generation (the highest-cost and most differentiated step), we evaluate running open-source models on owned infrastructure as we scale
- **Contract protections:** Our agreements with API providers include pricing lock-in provisions for the first 12 months
- **Budget reserves:** Financial model includes a 30% contingency buffer for AI cost increases

### Q7: What is the content moderation strategy? How do we prevent harmful AI-generated content?

All content goes through a three-layer moderation process before publishing:

1. **Generation-time guardrails:** Explicit content policies embedded in all AI prompts. Topics, themes, and content types are categorized as allowed, restricted (age-gate), or prohibited.

2. **Automated post-generation review:** Moderation models scan scripts and generated video frames for content policy violations, explicit material, and defamatory content.

3. **Human spot-check review:** A human editorial team reviews a random sample of published content weekly. Any policy-violating content identified is removed and used to improve generation guardrails.

**Long-term:** As volume scales, we invest in dedicated Trust & Safety infrastructure including content appeals process, creator policy education, and proactive abuse detection.

### Q8: How do we handle the chicken-and-egg problem with creators (if we build a creator platform)?

The creator platform launch strategy is:

1. **Supply before demand:** Recruit 50–100 "anchor creators" — romance novelists, fan fiction writers, drama podcasters — before opening the platform publicly. Pay these creators a guaranteed advance ($1,000–$5,000) for their first series to de-risk their participation.

2. **Curated launch:** The first cohort of creator content is handpicked for quality. Don't open the floodgates until quality bars are established.

3. **Creator tools before platform:** Release creator tools (script submission, AI preview, production estimation) 3 months before the public creator platform so early adopters can build familiarity.

4. **Proof of revenue:** The most powerful creator acquisition tool is proof that existing creators are earning money. Publish (with permission) creator earnings data from the first cohort.

### Q9: What does the international expansion strategy look like? How do we localize AI-generated content?

**AI Dubbing Strategy (Phase 1 — Year 1):**
- DramaSnap content produced in English
- AI dubbing using ElevenLabs or equivalent into Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Hindi, Indonesian
- Subtitle-only for Japanese, Korean, Chinese (markets with strong subtitle acceptance)
- Estimated cost per language version: $50–$150 per episode (AI voice synthesis + QA)

**Localized Production Strategy (Phase 2 — Year 2+):**
- Local language script generation using fine-tuned models on regional drama narrative styles
- Market-specific genre preferences (telenovela patterns for LATAM, K-drama patterns for Southeast Asia)
- Regional pricing adjusted to local purchasing power parity

| Latin America | Spanish/Portuguese | $1.8B | AI dubbing by Month 6 |

### Q10: What are the top 3 risks that could kill this business?

1. **AI content quality fails to meet consumer expectations at scale.** If AI video generation quality is not sufficiently good to generate positive NPS from paying subscribers, the business model collapses. Mitigation: Aggressive quality investment; human editorial overlay; don't scale user acquisition until quality metrics are validated.

2. **Incumbents move faster than expected.** If a well-capitalized player (ByteDance, Google, or ReelShort backed by a major studio) builds an AI production pipeline within 6 months, our window narrows significantly. Mitigation: Build proprietary advantages (data, fine-tuned models, creator relationships) that cannot be replicated even by a well-funded fast follower. Speed of execution is the primary mitigation.

3. **Regulatory risk from AI content legislation.** AI-generated content is an emerging regulatory area. New laws requiring disclosure, restricting AI video generation, or mandating human creative involvement could change our production model. Mitigation: Proactive legal engagement; maintain human editorial oversight that can be scaled up if required; monitor legislative activity in all key markets.
