# Andy Raskin's Strategic Narrative: DramaSnap

## Overview

Andy Raskin's Strategic Narrative framework argues that the best sales and marketing pitches are not product pitches — they are stories about a changing world. The structure follows five elements:
1. **The Big Change** — A macro shift that creates winners and losers
2. **Winners and Losers** — What happens to those who adapt vs. those who don't
3. **The Promised Land** — The new world that early adopters will reach
4. **Magic Gifts** — The capabilities your product provides to help customers win
5. **Evidence** — Proof that the Promised Land is achievable

This document applies this framework across four narrative contexts: investor pitch, elevator pitch, press release, and internal team rally.

---

## The Core Strategic Narrative

### Element 1: The Big Change

**The Three Converging Forces**

The entertainment industry is being reshaped by three simultaneous forces that, taken together, create the most significant shift in story consumption since the invention of television.

**Force 1: The Great Attention Fragmentation**

The average human attention span for digital content is declining, but more precisely, the contexts of consumption have fragmented. We no longer have "TV time" as a dedicated block. We have 47 micro-sessions per day — 3 minutes waiting for coffee, 12 minutes on the subway, 8 minutes before sleep. The problem is not that people don't want stories. The problem is that every story format was designed for contexts that no longer exist.

Netflix episodes are 45–60 minutes long. They were designed for the couch, not the commute. You cannot start a Netflix episode on your lunch break. You cannot finish one before your stop. Netflix is not wrong — it's just designed for a world of scheduled leisure time, and that world is disappearing for a huge portion of the population.

**Force 2: The Mobile-First Generation Is Now the Primary Consumer**

In 2024, for the first time, mobile overtook desktop as the primary screen for video content globally. More importantly, the demographic that grew up with mobile-first content (25–40 year olds today) is now in its peak earning and spending years. These consumers do not have a "phone mode" and a "TV mode." They have one screen — their phone — and they expect content to work perfectly on it.

The short drama format has already validated this shift. ReelShort, a platform of 60-second melodrama episodes, generated over $100M in revenue in 2023 from an audience that conventional entertainment industry wisdom said "didn't watch drama on their phones." They were wrong. The format just needed to be right.

**Force 3: AI Has Made Mass-Customization of Content Possible**

For the first time in history, the cost of producing video content is approaching zero. A story that would have cost $50,000 to produce with a human cast and crew can now be produced for $500 using AI-generated video, AI-synthesized voices, and AI-written scripts.

This is not a marginal cost improvement. This is a structural cost disruption — similar to what happened when digital printing disrupted physical newspapers, or when streaming disrupted DVD sales. The organizations built for the old cost structure will find it nearly impossible to adapt because their entire economics, organizational structures, and brand identities are built around expensive human production.

### Element 2: Winners and Losers

**The Losers: Those Who Cannot Adapt**

Organizations that will struggle in this new world:
- **Traditional streaming platforms (Netflix, Disney+):** Their brand identity is built on prestige human-produced content. They cannot produce AI drama without betraying their brand promise to creators and consumers. They are also protected from disruption by their existing subscribers — until those subscribers have a reason to choose something else for their mobile time.
- **Human-produced short drama platforms (ReelShort, ShortTV):** These platforms have proven the demand but are constrained by human production costs. They will fall behind any competitor who achieves AI production at scale because they cannot grow catalogs or personalize content at the same pace. They are also burdened by their coin-based monetization model, which creates friction and resentment at scale.
- **Traditional romance/drama fiction publishers:** Publishers who sell romance novels are seeing their audience migrate to serialized digital formats. Platforms like Wattpad are digital but text-only. The migration to video drama will accelerate as AI production quality improves.

**The Winners: Those Who Adapt Early**

- **Mobile-first drama consumers** who no longer have to choose between "satisfying story depth" and "content that fits my actual life"
- **Aspiring storytellers** who couldn't produce video content alone but can now bring their stories to life with AI tools
- **Platforms that build AI production capabilities now**, before incumbents develop the organizational capacity to respond
- **Advertisers and brands** who gain access to highly engaged audiences during mobile micro-sessions with premium content context

### Element 3: The Promised Land

**What the World Looks Like When You Win**

*For Drama Consumers:*

Imagine a morning commute where you are 43 episodes into a 100-episode drama that feels like it was written specifically for you. The male lead has a quiet strength you find compelling. The relationship arc is moving at the exact pace you like — slow-burn tension rather than immediate resolution. The setting is a world that appeals to your aesthetic. You finish two episodes on the train and save the next one as your reward for getting through the morning meeting. You've never paid per-episode coin charges. Your subscription is $6.99/month — less than a coffee. And there's a new episode of three other series you're following ready for you at lunch.

This is the Promised Land for the drama consumer: an infinite, personalized, mobile-native drama universe that fits perfectly into the actual shape of your life.

*For Content Creators:*

Imagine pitching a romance story idea on DramaSnap's creator platform and, three days later, seeing a 100-episode series produced from your outline — complete with characters, voices, and visuals — published to an audience of millions. Revenue sharing begins from episode 1. You are now a published serialized drama writer and series creator without a production budget, a network deal, or Hollywood connections.

*For the Platform:*

DramaSnap is the entertainment company of the AI era — producing 1,000 new episode-equivalents per day at a cost that would have required a $10M monthly production budget in 2023. The catalog grows faster than any human-produced platform can match. Personalization makes every user's feed feel curated. The creator economy generates a long tail of content that no editorial team could have commissioned. The platform becomes the first truly global short drama service, with content available in 20+ languages through AI dubbing.

### Element 4: Magic Gifts

**The Capabilities DramaSnap Provides**

Magic Gifts are the specific product capabilities that enable customers to reach the Promised Land. They should be named and described as transformative, not incremental.

**Gift 1: The AI Story Engine**

DramaSnap's AI content pipeline — powered by GPT-4 (scripts), Runway/Pika (video), and ElevenLabs (voice) — produces episodes at 10–40x lower cost than human production and grows the catalog continuously. For users, this means new content every day. For the platform, this means catalog growth that no incumbent can match.

*The transformation:* From "I've run out of things to watch" to "I'll never run out of things to watch."

**Gift 2: 60-Second Episodes, 100-Episode Arcs**

The format is not an arbitrary choice. 60 seconds is short enough to fit in any micro-session; 100 episodes is long enough to create the deep character investment and cliffhanger addiction of the best serialized drama. The combination is the only format that delivers both "snackable" consumption and "binge" satisfaction simultaneously.

*The transformation:* From "I don't have time for a drama" to "I just finished two episodes on the way to this meeting."

**Gift 3: Personalization That Compounds**

DramaSnap's recommendation and personalization engine learns from every second of viewing behavior. The more you watch, the more the platform understands what you love — and over time, the content feels increasingly curated for you specifically. In the future, the storylines themselves will adapt to your preference profile.

*The transformation:* From "I watch the same drama everyone else watches" to "This feels like someone wrote this for me."

**Gift 4: Mobile-First Design**

Every element of DramaSnap — episode length, visual composition, audio mixing, notification timing, offline download behavior — is designed for the phone screen and the mobile consumption context. This is not a web app ported to mobile. It is a mobile experience that happens to also work on a tablet.

*The transformation:* From "I have to wait until I'm home to watch something good" to "Every commute is a DramaSnap session."

### Element 5: Evidence

**Proof That the Promised Land Is Achievable**

**Market Validation Evidence:**
- ReelShort crossed $100M in annualized revenue by late 2023, proving that consumers will pay for 60-second drama on mobile
- Short-form video is the fastest-growing digital content category globally
- Mobile video accounts for 75%+ of all video consumption (Cisco Visual Networking Index)
- The global streaming market is projected to reach $330B by 2030

**AI Production Evidence:**
- AI video generation (Runway, Pika, Sora) has improved from "unusable" to "publishable quality" in 24 months
- ElevenLabs voice synthesis achieves near-human voice quality for scripted drama
- GPT-4 can produce drama scripts that pass editorial review quality bars
- Per-episode AI production costs are currently $200–$800 and declining

**Personalization Evidence:**
- Netflix reports that its recommendation engine is responsible for 80% of watched content
- Amazon's personalization engine drives 35% of total revenue
- Personalized content recommendation increases completion rate by 2–3x vs. generic recommendation (internal testing)

**Format Evidence:**
- The average DramaSnap session includes 5–8 episodes (5–8 minutes), which aligns perfectly with mobile micro-session duration data
- Episode completion rates for 60-second episodes are 40–60% higher than 90-second+ episodes
